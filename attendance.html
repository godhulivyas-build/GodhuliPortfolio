Data Analysis · Cohort Attendance & Grading

Attendance analysis — insights, trends, performance.

Given a term's worth of raw learner data, spread across five linked tabs, the task was to explain how attendance moved through the term, compute a fair comprehensive final grade for every learner, and turn both into recommendations backed by the numbers, not opinion.

Problem Statement

Three questions, one dataset.

The brief: analyze the learner data provided across the workbook and answer three questions, output as a clean, formatted spreadsheet, using formulas throughout so every answer traces back to a source cell.

The dashboard view built from the analysis

1. How does learner attendance change over the course of the term? What factors influence the trend?

2. Calculate the final grade (AA, A, B, C, etc.) for every learner — a comprehensive read of performance across subjects, not just one quiz.

3. Recommend how to improve attendance and scores, with the data backing every recommendation.

Workbook Overview

Five tabs, one linked model.

The raw data arrived split across five tabs; the analysis lives in formulas that pull across all of them rather than in pasted values.

Approach

What the dashboard actually shows.

Attendance trend

  • Attendance by session type (Practical, Theory, Workshop) to see where drop-off concentrates
  • Attendance by delivery mode (online vs. offline) as a likely driver of the trend
  • Read against the term timeline, not just an overall average

Final grading

  • Post-session quiz scores rolled up per learner across all subjects
  • Absentee scores correctly carry their 5-mark deduction before aggregation
  • Aggregate mapped to the relative grading bands (AA, A, B, C…) from the Grading Criteria tab

Recommendations

What the data says to change.

  1. Fix the weak session types first. Attendance is not flat across session types — whichever type shows the steepest drop (workshops, in this dataset) is where scheduling, timing or format should change first, since fixing the largest gap moves the average the most.
  2. Treat mode as a lever, not a constant. With attendance split nearly evenly between online and offline, the format that shows the better attendance-to-score correlation should be favoured for sessions the data shows learners are most likely to skip.
  3. Target interventions at the bottom of the grade curve, not the whole cohort. Because the deduction for absent-but-attempted quizzes is already baked in, low final grades that also carry low attendance are the clearest, most defensible group to prioritise for outreach.
