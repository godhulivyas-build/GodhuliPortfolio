Data Analysis · Growth Accounting

Growth accounting — where the growth actually comes from.

A top-line "users are up" chart hides more than it shows. This teardown decomposes weekly active users into new, retained and resurrected users, then uses the Quick Ratio to check whether that growth is healthy or just masking churn underneath.

The Chart

Every bar, decomposed.

Each bar is one week's Weekly Active Users, split into new users (first time active), retained users (active in consecutive weeks), and resurrected users (returning after a gap) — so the chart shows what's driving growth, not just the trend line.

Weekly growth accounting chart

Data & Method

Device-level activity, rolled up weekly.

The source data is device-level: one record per unique device active in a given week. Duplicate (Device_ID, Week) records were removed before any calculation, so the WAU count is never inflated.

Key fields

  • Device_ID — unique user/device identifier
  • Week / Week_Num — week label and chronological index
  • First_Week — first week the device appeared
  • Is_New_User — helper flag for first-time users

Core identity

The whole model rests on one accounting identity, computed per week:

WAU(t) = New + Retained + Resurrected

Metric Definitions & Formulas

Five metrics, each traceable to a formula.

Weekly Active Users

Pivot table

Rows: Week · Values: count of unique Device_ID.

New Users

Helper column

Users whose current week equals their first-ever active week, then filtered where Is_New_User = 1.

=IF(Week_Num = First_Week, 1, 0)

Retained Users

Derived

Users active this week who are not new.

Retained = WAU − New

Resurrected Users

Derived

Users returning after a gap in activity. Negative values mean no resurrection that week, floored at zero.

Resurrected = MAX(0, WAU − New − Retained)

Churned Users

Derived

Users active last week who did not return this week.

Churned = MAX(0, WAU(prev week) − Retained)

Quick Ratio

Health check

Whether growth is outrunning churn, or just offsetting it.

Quick Ratio = (New + Resurrected) / Churned

Reading the Quick Ratio

~1–1.2, in this dataset.

The scale below is the read on any Quick Ratio value — and where this cohort actually landed.

< 1 · Declining
≈ 1 · Flat / fragile
> 1 · Growing

↑ This cohort sits at ~1–1.2 — just inside "growing," but barely. New and resurrected users are only marginally outpacing churn.

Business Insight & Recommendation

Growth is real, but it's borrowed.

Trajectory

Moderate, inconsistent growth — positive overall but not compounding week over week. Early traction, limited stickiness.

Drivers

Driven mostly by new acquisition. Churn stays consistently high; retention is stable but flat, not improving — weak habit formation.

Resurrection

Sporadic, suggesting some latent value in the product — but nowhere near enough to offset churn on its own.

Recommendation: Fix retention before scaling acquisition. Reducing early-stage churn would lift the Quick Ratio directly, letting new-user growth compound instead of leaking out the bottom.

Concretely: onboarding, early activation, and repeat-usage triggers — the three levers most likely to convert a first session into a habit.
