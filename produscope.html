Product Requirements Document · Multi-Agent Financial Planning

Produscope AI — "can I afford this?"
answered honestly, five agents at a time.

"Can I afford this?" is really four questions stacked on top of each other: what do I save, what do I already spend, what would this cost me, and what does it delay. One long prompt handles that badly. Produscope splits it across five narrow specialist agents and a coordinator that reconciles their output — and it never asks for bank or UPI access to do it.

Architecture

Five narrow remits, one reconciler.

Each agent's job is small enough that its output can be checked independently — that constraint is the entire reason the decomposition works, and the same principle later became the judge layer at CupShup.

Three Surfaces

One question, three ways to ask it.

Dashboard

Where am I now

Savings, surplus and runway, read at a glance — the baseline every other surface reasons from.

Simulator

What if

"Can I afford an iPhone 16" resolves to a savings-after number, a plain yes or no, and the delay it causes to an existing goal — never just a chart to interpret.

Goals Tracker

Am I on track

ETAs computed against actual surplus, not motivational rounding — if the number moved, the date moves with it.

The Deliberate Constraint

No bank connection. No UPI linkage. On purpose.

Manual entry only, everywhere in the product — which costs convenience and automatic categorisation, on the bet that an Indian user will try a finance tool that never asks for account access.

Trade-off, stated plainly: less auto-categorisation and more manual upkeep, in exchange for a product that can say, truthfully, that it has never seen your bank account.
