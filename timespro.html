The Model

It's not a filter. It's a weighted average.

There's no permutation search over "all combinations of course × answer" — that would explode combinatorially for no benefit. Instead, every course gets scored against your answers on 6 independent factors, each factor contributes a fixed share of the final number, and the shares add up to exactly 100%. It's the same logic as a report card weighting a final exam more than a homework set.

Per-Factor Scoring

Three different ways to score a "match," not one.

Treating every factor as a simple yes/no would throw away information — a 5-10 year professional isn't a total mismatch for a 2-5 year course the way a total beginner is. So each factor type gets the scoring logic that fits its actual shape.

Exact-match with a soft miss

Industry · Goal · Skill · Format

If your answer is in the course's accepted list, it scores 100. If not, it doesn't score zero — it takes a fixed penalty specific to that factor, because a skill mismatch should hurt more than an industry mismatch.

score = (answer in course.acceptedList) ? 100 : missPenalty

Miss penalties, in this build: skill 25, goal 35, industry 40, format 45 — the tighter the penalty, the more that factor's mismatch actually matters.

Ordinal distance

Experience Level

Experience isn't a category, it's a position on a line: Student → 0-2 → 2-5 → 5-10 → 10+ years. An exact band match scores 100; being one band off costs 30 points, two bands off costs 60, with a floor so it never reads as a total disqualification.

score = inRange ? 100 : max(20, 100 − distance × 30)

Range fit

Investment Readiness

Budget readiness is a 0–100 slider, not a category, and a course has a target range rather than one value. Inside the range: full score. Below it: a graceful penalty proportional to the gap. Above it — more budget than needed — barely penalised at all, since "can spend more" is rarely a real mismatch.

score = inRange ? 100 : (below ? max(20, 100 − gap) : 90)

The Flow

One profile, six scorers, one ranking.

All six scorers run independently and in parallel against the same profile — nothing here is sequential or conditional. The only combination step is the weighted sum at the end.

Why This Shape, Not a "Smarter" One

No LLM, no permutation search — on purpose.

Why not brute-force permutations

With 6 factors and up to 6 options each, an exhaustive "score every combination" approach scores nothing extra — the visitor only ever has one actual profile. What matters is scoring that one profile against every course correctly, which is O(courses × factors), not combinatorial.

Why not an LLM call

This runs live at an event, on a booth screen, with people queued behind the visitor. A weighted-sum calculation is instant and deterministic — the same answers always produce the same ranking, which matters when the output is printed onto a physical Future Career Card.

The actual difficulty wasn't the math. It was picking miss-penalties and ranges that felt fair across genuinely different course catalogues — a beginner-friendly bootcamp and an executive program shouldn't be scored by the same experience curve, so each course carries its own accepted ranges rather than one global rule.
