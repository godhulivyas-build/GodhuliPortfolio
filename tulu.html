Associate Product Manager Case Submission · Tulu Health App

The problem isn't acquisition. It's the first five minutes.

Installs were up 23% and the team wanted more acquisition. The data said something else: the share of users making it from install to first activity had fallen from 42% to 26%, a 37% relative decline. I built a working prototype of the fix instead of a slide about it.

Executive Diagnosis

Installs went up. Everything after install went down.

Thesis: the problem is not acquisition, it's the first-value experience. Signup completion and first-activity completion both deteriorated, while retention and NPS declined downstream. The first priority isn't more acquisition — it's finding exactly where users fail to reach first value, then testing an onboarding fix against that failure point.

What the data says

  • Acquisition: volume up 23%, but installs rising doesn't mean acquisition is healthy — quality is unverified, and fitness (the largest new-user source) also has the weakest retention, so the mix itself may be shifting.
  • Activation: the largest relative deterioration in the entire funnel — 42% to 26% — the single biggest lever in the dataset.
  • Retention: declines downstream of activation, consistent with, but not yet proof of, activation being the upstream driver.

Key questions still open

  • Has the acquisition mix shifted toward lower-retention segments like fitness over this period?
  • Where exactly in onboarding do users drop — one screen, one step, or distributed friction?
  • Does the reported 2.4x retention relationship survive de-duplication and cohort segmentation?

Root Cause Analysis

Three hypotheses, ranked by confidence, not by preference.

HypothesisConfidenceReasoning
Onboarding friction is suppressing activationHighSignup completion and first-activity completion deteriorated together in the same period — consistent with a structural funnel problem, not isolated noise.
Acquisition mix has shifted toward lower-retention segmentsMediumFitness is the largest acquisition segment and has the weakest retention; if its install share grew, blended metrics would worsen even with an unchanged product.
Technical or performance friction is compounding the lossNeeds validationNot directly evidenced yet, but performance issues are cheap to check and would compound any onboarding friction if present.

Data Integrity Risk

Caught before it drove a decision.

The reported 2.4× retention relationship should not yet be used for product prioritisation. Android duplicates activity events in an estimated 8–12% of sessions, so activity-based metrics may be inflated.

Before treating the 2.4× relationship as causal, in order: (1) de-duplicate activity events, (2) recalculate the relationship on clean data, (3) segment by acquisition channel, platform and cohort to rule out survivorship, and (4) only run a controlled intervention if the relationship still holds after steps 1–3.

Same discipline applied to the headline "paid conversion 4.8% to 5.1%" — it depends entirely on a denominator the brief never specifies. If measured on activated users only, absolute payers likely fell; if measured on all signups, they likely grew. The denominator gets confirmed before this is reported as a win.

Proposed Intervention

Not a full app. One journey, five screens.

I deliberately did not build a full app. I built one focused, high-leverage journey: get a new user from install to a completed, meaningful first activity with as little friction as possible. The principle — don't add more content, reduce the distance between opening the app and experiencing value.

1

Welcome

Set expectation in one line, single CTA.

Design choice: no feature list, no home-screen dump on first open.
2

Goal selection

"What would you like help with today?" (sleep, fitness, stress, general)

Design choice: cards, not a form — reduces choice friction at the highest-risk step.
3

Recommended first activity

One matched activity, not a catalog.

Design choice: removes the choice overload identified in H1.
4

Activity completion

"Nice work, you're off to a start," plus a progress signal.

Design choice: confirms value was delivered, not just that a screen was completed.
5

Next step

One lightweight next action, not a full plan.

Design choice: avoids overwhelming a user who just had one small win.

A working clickable prototype of this flow, built in Lovable, ships with an instrumentation layer and a Control/Treatment toggle, so the onboarding experiment below can be demoed, not just described.

Why not an AI coach first. Tulu's platform is built around AI agents and measurable outcomes, which made it tempting to lead with an AI wellness coach. I deliberately didn't. The data identifies an activation problem, not an intelligence problem. AI should be introduced where it removes a demonstrated bottleneck, not because AI itself is the solution. Once the First Value Path is working, AI becomes a way to personalise that journey — not a standalone feature to bolt on first.

Metrics

One North Star, one fake-win trap to guard against.

Weekly Engaged Users (WEU)

North Star

Unique users completing at least one meaningful core activity on 2+ days within a rolling 7-day period. The exact definition gets validated against the app's primary user value proposition before being finalised.

Activation rate

Primary

% of new users completing the first meaningful activity within the first X days — using the existing first-activity event as the initial definition since it's already instrumented, and only introducing a deeper habit metric (e.g. 3 activities in 5 days) after validating that behaviour predicts D30 retention.

Opt-out, uninstall, crash rate

Guardrails

Protect against solving activation by degrading trust or stability.

Experiment: Onboarding Redesign

Sized before launch, not adjusted after.

Hypothesis

If we reduce onboarding choices and guide users directly to their first meaningful activity, more new users will reach first value without negatively affecting D7 retention or notification opt-outs.

Primary & secondary metrics

  • Primary: first meaningful activity completion
  • Secondary: time to first activity, signup completion, D7 and D30 retention

Sample size, determined before launch: from baseline activation rate, minimum detectable lift, 95% confidence and 80% power — not assumed in advance. The experiment runs to that pre-set sample size rather than stopping early on a promising interim result.

Prioritisation · 12 Engineer-Weeks

What ships, what waits, and why.

P0

Onboarding simplification

Directly addresses the largest observed funnel loss, install to first activity, and reduces friction across the entire experience at low risk.

P1

Performance fixes

High potential, sequenced after onboarding data confirms the real friction point.

P2

Notification experiment

A re-engagement lever, but only after understanding why users disengage in the first place.

Defer

Personalised home screen, weekly plan; AI wellness coach

High implementation cost and insufficient evidence that lack of AI guidance is the current growth constraint — not a rejection of the idea, a sequencing call.

User Research

Four questions, asked before building more.

  • 1After your first session, what did you expect to happen next?
  • 2Was there a moment where you weren't sure what to do next?
  • 3What were you trying to accomplish when you opened the app?
  • 4What almost made you leave?

CEO Challenge · "Referrals Now"

Fast and free isn't the same as smart, yet.

Referrals feel fast and free, but only 26 of 100 installs reach trying the product today, down from 42. Referring more people into that same funnel means losing most of them too, and spending goodwill and reward budget on users who churn anyway.

The call: fix onboarding first, re-measure the funnel in 3–4 weeks, then launch referrals into a funnel that actually converts — a better return on the same budget. If timing is non-negotiable, a small capped referral pilot alongside the onboarding fix avoids scaling spend into a leaky bucket.

Post-Launch Decision

Partial win. Do not roll back.

Positive

  • Behaviour +18%
  • D7 retention +6%
  • Paid conversion +2%

Unresolved

  • D30 retention unchanged

Negative

  • NPS −3
  • Opt-outs +14%

Decision: keep the onboarding redesign, isolate the notification component, and run a follow-up experiment reducing notification frequency. The goal is to preserve the D7 improvement while recovering NPS and opt-outs — not to ship-and-forget, and not to panic-revert.

Next 30/60/90 Days

What happens, in order.

Day 0–30

Ship the de-duplication fix; launch the onboarding A/B test; begin a 12-interview research pass.

Day 30–60

Read out the onboarding experiment at its first checkpoint; run the notification-cadence follow-up if the post-launch pattern repeats.

Day 60–90

Decide ship / iterate / kill on onboarding; revisit the personalised home screen and AI-assisted personalisation only once activation is confirmed fixed.
