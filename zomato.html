Zomato — boosting orders for
new and small restaurants.

A rating-sorted marketplace has a cold-start problem built into its ranking: a new or small restaurant with genuinely good food has no reviews, no order history, and no way to out-rank an established name on the default feed. The strategy, and later a working prototype, is built around fixing that specific failure.

The Problem

Good food, zero visibility.

The default sort on any food delivery app rewards restaurants that already have volume: more orders produce more ratings, which produce more visibility, which produce more orders. A new listing starts every one of those loops at zero, regardless of food quality.

For the restaurant, this means the first weeks after joining the platform are the hardest, exactly when they can least afford low order volume. For the platform, it means a shrinking base of newly onboarded restaurants ever break through, which quietly narrows the marketplace to whoever was already winning.

Strategy

Don't fix the ranking. Build a second door.

Rather than trying to out-game an algorithm tuned for scale, the strategy adds a separate, parallel discovery surface built specifically for restaurants that don't have volume yet — and gives users a reason to walk through that door.

Pillar 01

Trust, not stars

New restaurants get a "New · Verified" badge instead of an empty star rating, so the absence of reviews reads as "just joined," not "unproven."

Pillar 02

First-order economics

Steep, front-loaded discounts (30% off the first 50 orders, buy-2-get-1) absorb the acquisition cost onto the platform for a short window, not onto the new restaurant's thin margins.

Pillar 03

Hyperlocal framing

A city-specific edition surfaces what a citywide app buries: late-night options, student-priced picks, and "local favourites" that only make sense at neighbourhood scale.

Discovery

A "New & Trusted" home row

Sits above the default feed, not buried in a filter menu — the first thing a user sees is the newest listings, each with a live first-order incentive attached.

Retention loop

Food Community

Users tag photos to a restaurant under #UjjainEats and earn badges for doing it — the review problem is solved by making early advocacy a game, not a chore.

The badge set rewards exactly the behaviour the strategy needs: people trying and vouching for restaurants before the algorithm would have surfaced them on its own.

Why It Placed

The judged strength, and the honest gap.

What the case study got right

It reframed a ranking problem as a discovery-surface problem, which sidesteps rebuilding Zomato's core algorithm entirely. The fix ships as a new section of the app, not a rewrite of how every restaurant is ranked.

What I'd want real data on

The discount economics only work if the lift in repeat orders from a "New & Trusted" placement outweighs the margin given up in the first-order window — a number the case study format doesn't let you test.
